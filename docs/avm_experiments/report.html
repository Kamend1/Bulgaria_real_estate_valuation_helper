Why the model was wrong, and what actually fixes it

EDA, data cleaning, target-transform, scaling, and LightGBM hyperparameter experiments across all 5 property segments — run against the live 263K-listing database.

01

Headline result

One change explains almost everything: excluding foreign listings and implausible-area rows, then trimming the extreme 0.5% tails of the target, before any modeling happens. It rescued industrial from a broken model, stabilized residential's wildly inconsistent validation scores, and improved every segment. Hyperparameter tuning added a further, smaller gain — mainly for residential.

02

Root cause: two concrete data bugs, not vague "outliers"

Area-parsing failures produce absurd price/m² values8 rows at exactly 1.00 m²

The single worst offender in the entire residential set: a Halkidiki villa listed at €310,000 with area_sqm_model = 1.00 — giving a "price per m²" of €310,000. Six more of the top 15 residential listings by price/m² share the identical area = 1.00 signature. This isn't market variance; it's the area parser falling back to a placeholder when it can't extract a real number.

Foreign vacation homes mixed into the Bulgarian residential set1,054 rows, 0.73%

Mostly Greek Halkidiki listings (already tagged geo_category = 'foreign' by the existing pipeline, just never excluded from training). A domestic Bulgarian appraisal tool has no business learning Greek coastal pricing as part of its Sofia/Plovdiv/Varna price surface — this is a scope decision, not only a statistical one.

Combined effect on residential's target distributionskewness 87.3 → 0.77

Before cleaning, residential price/m² had a skewness coefficient of 87.3 — a distribution dominated by a handful of extreme values, not a genuinely long-tailed market. After removing foreign rows, the area≤15m² rows, and trimming the remaining 0.5%/99.5% tails (2,550 rows total, 1.78%), skewness dropped to 0.77 — mildly right-skewed, which is exactly what a real, healthy housing-price distribution should look like. Every other segment showed the same pattern (retail 7.1→1.3, industrial 47.3→1.8, office 4.5→0.9, hospitality 2.0→1.5).

03

Why cross-validation, not a single split

The production models (deployed before this investigation) were evaluated on one random 60/20/20 stratified split. Residential's validation R² came out at 0.11 while its test R² came out at 0.60 — the same model, two wildly different scores depending on which fold a handful of extreme rows happened to land in. That instability was the first clue something was structurally wrong, not just "needs more tuning."

This round switched to 5-fold cross-validation, reporting mean ± standard deviation across folds. The std itself is diagnostic: residential's fold-to-fold R² std on uncleaned data was ±0.121 — huge instability, matching the earlier single-split symptom exactly. On cleaned data, that std collapsed to ±0.005. That collapse in variance is the strongest evidence in this whole investigation that the cleaning step, not the model or its hyperparameters, was the real problem.

Recommendations

apply now
Add the 3-step cleaning pass to scripts/train_avm_model.pyExclude geo_category = 'foreign', enforce a segment-specific minimum area, trim the target's 0.5%/99.5% tails — before the stratified split. This is the highest-confidence, lowest-effort change here: it fixed every segment, most dramatically industrial.
apply now
Adopt the tuned hyperparameters found per segmentConcrete params listed above for each. Biggest payoff for residential (only there did tuning move R² meaningfully); for the other four, treat it as a small free win rather than the main event.
skip / defer
Don't adopt log-transform as a blanket defaultOnce cleaned, none of the 5 segments showed a consistent, meaningful MAE improvement from log1p — the perceived skew was mostly the outlier contamination, not real. If anything is kept, let the training script pick B vs. C by CV score per segment automatically rather than hardcoding one choice.
confirmed, no action
Feature scaling is a non-issue for LightGBM, as expectedConfig C vs. D differed only at noise level in all 5 segments — empirically confirms tree models don't need standardized numeric inputs. No further work here.
future work
Re-run full GridSearchCV tuning only periodically, not on every retrain4 of 5 segments saw tuning cost far outweigh its benefit once data was clean. Reserve the ~19-minute tuning pass for a quarterly cadence; let routine retrains reuse the last tuned params with fresh data.
future work
Consider target/mean-encoding for neighborhoodtitle_geo_2_model has 300–3,100 distinct values depending on segment — one-hot spreads that signal thin. Not tested in this round; a plausible next lever specifically for residential and retail.
06

Honest caveats

These are imot.bg asking prices, not closed transaction prices — some MAE floor is structural and won't move no matter how much cleaning or tuning happens. Residential's 43% within-±10% (up from 36%) is a real improvement, not a finished AVM. Hospitality and, to a lesser extent, office and industrial are working with a few thousand rows or fewer — their CV std bands are wide enough that month-to-month retraining will show real number movement that isn't model regression, just small-sample noise. And this was one random seed per config — the relative story (cleaning >> tuning >> transform/scaling) is robust, but exact numbers would shift a few points under a different seed.

Exploratory run only — models/avm/, avm_models table, and scripts/train_avm_model.py were not modified. Full harness and raw results.json available in the session scratchpad.
